The answer

Disruption is up. Volume is not the cause, and equipment reliability is flat

Service volumeNoPlanned km fell 6% (2019 to 2022); major incidents per million km rose 50% (2019 to 2023, Jan-Sep)
Reporting practicesPartlyRaw counts include no-delay incidents; Apr-Sep 2025 runs 15-52% short; duration and clock disagree on 1.2% → 2.3% of train incidents
ReliabilityDisruption upMajor train incidents 258 (2019) → 365 (2024). Customer-caused 126 → 222; rolling stock 47 → 46

Classification does not drive the trend: major incidents are counted from incident type and duration category alone.

Before reading any trend

Read raw, the file points the wrong way three times

Station incidents are not interruptions

36% of the file. The STM states they do not affect metro service.

Station incidents get no cause

By design: the STM uses the symptom as their cause, so the cause field only describes train incidents.

2025 is not an improvement

Train incidents are published late. April-September 2025 is incomplete and left out.

Everything that follows uses train incidents only.
What changed, 2019-2024

Major incidents per km rose 50% by 2023

  • To 2022, the major share rose because minor incidents fell. Major ones held at 247-273 a year
  • From 2023, major incidents rose: 335 in 2023, 365 in 2024
  • The line driving it changes: green in early 2023, orange in 2024 (104 → 153)
Planned km ends 2023-09, so 2023 is compared on Jan-Sep and 2024 is counts only. Source: notebooks/02_reliability.ipynb
Where the rise comes from

The rise is customer-caused; rolling stock is flat

  • Customer-caused: 126 (2019) → 222 (2024). Deliberate mischief alone 62 → 152
  • Rolling stock flat; fixed equipment varies, then rises in 2024
  • Cause is set weeks later, but the symptom, recorded at declaration, shows the same rise

How to measure reliability

Track major incidents per million km, split controllable vs external

  • A rate, not a count: major train incidents per million km, by line, monthly
  • Two parts: controllable (rolling stock, fixed equipment, operations) and external (customer). They have moved differently since 2022
  • Station incidents stay out of the service measure: they carry no delay
  • Hold back months still filling in: train incidents arrive 30+ days late
  • 30+ min events as a rate with an interval: about 70 a year
How to prioritize action

Each stakeholder has a different next step

VP Operations

Target customer-caused incidents, mostly deliberate mischief, not equipment. Watch the monthly rate by line: the line driving it changes. Use the symptom x location table to order incidents at declaration.

CFO

This data cannot measure financial burden: no cost, no ridership. Usable proxy: delay-minutes by line and year, weighted by peak hours.

Public Affairs / Safety

Quote 30+ min events as rates with intervals. Do not quote 2025 figures yet. The triage table does not single out severe events.

How to improve data collection

Five data fixes would answer what this data cannot

  • Record the exact delay in minutes, and document the duration field (missing from the data dictionary)
  • Timestamp each flag (emergency brake, material damage, lines affected): what limits prediction most
  • Publish km after September 2023, and actual km next to planned km
  • Add ridership and a cost per incident, for the CFO and Public Affairs questions
  • Mark which months are complete
What this data cannot tell you

Four questions stay open until the data changes

  • Cost and rider impact: no cost, ridership or station volume data
  • Per-km rates after September 2023: the 2024 findings are counts
  • Why customer-caused incidents rose: needs ridership and security data
  • Whether causes are recorded consistently: they are assigned by a later analysis
